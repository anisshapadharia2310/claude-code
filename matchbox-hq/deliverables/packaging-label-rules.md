# What Must Be Printed On Your Matchbox

**Written 12 Sep 2026. Links checked 12 Sep 2026.**

## The rule in one sentence

The moment you put your brand and a price on a box and sell it, it is a
**"pre-packaged commodity"** and the **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities)
Rules, 2011** tell you exactly what must be printed on it.

This is not optional and it is not expensive. It is just ink.
Get it right on the first print run, because reprinting 500 boxes is not free.

**Main rule:** Rule 6 of the LMPC Rules, 2011.
Source: https://1-comply.com/faqs-on-legal-metrology-act-and-packaged-commodities-rules/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://www.omgarudagroup.com/blogs/mandatory-labeling-requirements-under-lmpc-what-must-appear-on-pre-packaged-goods (seen 12 Sep 2026)

---

## The 8 things that MUST be on the pack

### 1. Name and address of the brand owner
You are not the manufacturer. The Sivakasi factory is.
But your name is on the box, so **you are legally on the hook**.

The accepted wording is **"Marketed by:"** followed by your full name and complete address.
Recent clarifications confirm that the brand owner's name and address with the words
**"Marketed by"** or **"Brand Owner"** satisfies Rule 6(1)(a).

> **Read this twice:** if your brand and address are on the pack as marketer,
> **you become the "deemed manufacturer"** and any action for a label violation
> comes to *you*, not the factory.
> Source: https://1-comply.com/faqs-on-legal-metrology-act-and-packaged-commodities-rules/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**"Complete address" means a real, full postal address with PIN code.**
Not just "Pune". Not just an Instagram handle.
If that scares you, use a relative's address or a small commercial address. It must be real.

### 2. Name and address of the manufacturer / packer
Where the manufacturer is different from the packer, both are declared.
Practically: print **"Manufactured by: [Factory name, Sivakasi, Tamil Nadu]"**.

**Ask your supplier for the exact legal name + address they want printed.**
Do this in the same message as your price enquiry. It costs you nothing.

### 3. Common or generic name of the product
Print: **Safety Matches**.
Not "fire sticks", not your clever product name. The plain generic name must be there.
Your brand name can be as big and cool as you like *in addition*.

### 4. Net quantity
Matches are sold **by number**, not by weight.
Print: **Net Quantity: 50 Matchsticks (N)** — or whatever the real count is.

**Count it yourself from the sample before you print.** Sivakasi counts vary
(commonly 35–50 per box). If the box says 50 and contains 40, that is an offence.
Ask the factory for the **guaranteed minimum count**, and print that number, not the average.

### 5. MRP — inclusive of all taxes
The wording matters. Print it as:
**MRP ₹ [XX] (incl. of all taxes)** — or "Maximum Retail Price ₹XX inclusive of all taxes".

- It must be the **maximum** price. You can sell for less. You can never sell for more.
- It must **include GST**. You cannot print "₹20 + GST".
- If you sell a pack of 5 boxes, the **pack** carries its own MRP.

### 6. Month and year of packing
Print: **Packed: [MM/YYYY]** — e.g. `Packed: 10/2026`.
Month and year is enough. You do not need a day.
Matches do not expire, so **no "best before" is needed**.

### 7. Consumer care details
A real name/designation, a **phone number**, and an **email address** where a customer
can complain. Print:
**Customer Care: [name], [phone], [email]**

Use a business email, not your personal one. A free Gmail is fine on day one.

### 8. Country of origin
Print: **Country of Origin: India**.
Easy — your matches are made in Sivakasi.
(This one is getting stricter for *imported* goods on e-commerce. Not your problem.)

## Two more things — not Legal Metrology, but do them anyway

### 9. Safety / handling text
**I could not find a government rule forcing specific warning words onto a plain
safety matchbox in India.** The Indian Standard is **IS 2653:2004 "Safety Matches in
Boxes"**, and I could not open its marking clause — the file was blocked from this machine.
Source: https://www.services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/bisconnect/standard_review/Standard_review/Isdetails?ID=OTE3NA%3D%3D (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**VERIFY WITH A CA / your supplier — my best understanding is that the safety text on
Indian matchboxes is industry convention and product-liability sense, not a printed statute.**

**Print it anyway.** It costs nothing, it looks professional, and it is your defence
if a child gets hurt. Standard text used across the industry:

> **CAUTION: Keep away from children. Close box before striking.
> Strike away from body. Store in a dry place.**

Ask your Sivakasi supplier what *they* normally print. They have done this for decades.

### 10. Batch / lot number
Not strictly required for a non-food item like this, but a simple **`Batch: 01`**
lets you trace a bad run back to one order. Free. Do it.

## Size of the printing — the bit that will actually bite you

A matchbox face is roughly **5.5 cm x 3.5 cm ≈ 19 cm²**.

Because that principal display panel is **under 50 cm²**, the minimum height for the
declaration letters and numerals is **1.0 mm**.
Source: https://nkgabc.com/principal-display-panel-how-to-declare-information-on-a-package/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

Also: **letter width must be at least one-third of its height.**
So no ultra-condensed hipster fonts on the legal text. Keep the legal block plain.

**Practical translation for your designer:**
- Legal text ≈ **4pt minimum**, and 4pt is genuinely tiny. Aim for **5–6pt** to be safe.
- Put the **entire legal block on the BACK face** of the box.
- Keep the **front face** purely for the brand and the art. That is allowed.
- Do not let the designer make the legal text grey-on-grey. It must be legible.

## One honest uncertainty

There is an exemption (Rule 26) for very small packages — net weight **10 g or less**
**"if sold by weight or measure"**.
Source: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/faqs-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-2011.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)

A matchbox weighs about that much. **But matches are sold by NUMBER, not by weight**,
so my reading is that **this exemption does not save you**.

**VERIFY WITH A CA or the Pune Legal Metrology office — my best understanding is that
you must print the full set of declarations.**

**It does not matter much either way: print everything.** The cost of printing the legal
block is ₹0. The cost of a Legal Metrology penalty is not.

# LABEL TEMPLATE — hand this to your designer

## FRONT FACE (the pretty one)

```
        [BRAND NAME]
        [logo / artwork / your Gen Z design]

        Safety Matches
```

Only two rules on the front: the words **Safety Matches** must appear somewhere legible,
and the brand must not claim anything false. Everything else is yours to play with.

## BACK FACE (the legal one) — copy this block exactly

```
SAFETY MATCHES
Net Qty: [50] Matchsticks (N)
MRP ₹[XX.00] (incl. of all taxes)
Packed: [MM/YYYY]        Batch: [01]

Marketed by: [YOUR FULL NAME / BRAND TRADING NAME]
[Flat/Shop no., Street]
[Area], Pune, Maharashtra - [PIN CODE]

Manufactured by: [FACTORY LEGAL NAME]
[Address], Sivakasi, Tamil Nadu - [PIN]

Country of Origin: India

Customer Care: [Your Name]
[+91 XXXXX XXXXX] | [hello@yourbrand.in]

CAUTION: Keep away from children.
Close box before striking. Strike away
from body. Store in a dry place.
```

## SIDE / STRIKER FACES
Free space. Art, a line of copy, a QR code to your Instagram, a slogan. No rules.

## Designer instructions — paste this to them

> Back face legal block: minimum 1.0 mm cap height (use 5–6pt to be safe).
> Letter width at least 1/3 of letter height — no condensed fonts in this block.
> High contrast, dark text on light ground or vice versa. Do not tint it back.
> Do not rearrange or delete any line. Do not abbreviate the addresses.
> Front face is yours. Back face is legally fixed.

## Before you send it to print — final check

- [ ] Have you **counted the sticks** in the sample box? Does the number match?
- [ ] Have you got the factory's **exact legal name and address in writing**?
- [ ] Is the MRP a number you can live with? **It includes GST.**
- [ ] Is the customer care phone number one you will actually answer?
- [ ] Is the address a real postal address with a PIN code?
- [ ] Is every legal line still readable when printed at actual size? **Print one at home first.**

# What changes for Amazon / Flipkart / Blinkit

**The box stays the same. The listing page gets extra work.**

Under **Rule 6(10)** of the LMPC Rules, an e-commerce entity must make sure the Rule 6(1)
declarations are **displayed on the listing itself** — not just on the physical pack.
Source: https://ssrana.in/articles/legal-metrology-compliance-for-e-commerce-businesses/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

So on each marketplace listing you must type in:
- Name and address of the manufacturer / packer / marketer
- Net quantity (number of sticks)
- MRP inclusive of all taxes
- Country of origin
- Consumer care contact

Marketplaces enforce this. They have dedicated fields for it. Fill them in honestly —
mismatches between listing and box are a common reason listings get pulled.

**Also on marketplaces:**
- **GST is effectively mandatory.** Amazon and Flipkart will not switch on a seller
  account for taxable goods without a GSTIN. See `legal-checklist.md` item 7.
- **You will need an HSN code.** Matches are **HSN 3605**.
- **Matches are restricted for air shipping and some courier partners.**
  This is a logistics blocker, not a legal one — the Launch agent is chasing it.
- **A 2026 amendment** adds a country-of-origin searchable filter requirement for
  **imported** products from 1 Jul 2026. Yours are Indian, so this does not hit you.
  Source: https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/21/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2026-explained/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Your own website:** the same information should appear on the product page.
It is good practice and it is the same copy-paste. Do it from day one.
