## READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE — the thing most likely to bite you

**You are asking Sivakasi vendors for pink and black match heads and dyed black sticks.**
(That's in your own enquiry message — `supplier-outreach.md`.)

Indian law draws a line between **"safety matches"** and **"colour matches"**.
Colour matches sit near fireworks. Safety matches don't.
Source: https://www.comply4hr.com/docs/tn/tnfr/TNFR95SXXIV.htm (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://ssrana.in/articles/fireworks-law-in-india/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

My reading: **pigment in the head of an ordinary safety match is still a safety match.**
A "colour match" in the legal sense is one that **burns with a coloured flame** —
Bengal/star matches. That is a different animal and a different rulebook.

**But I could not confirm this from PESO directly — their FAQ PDF is blocked from this machine.**

**So ask every vendor this one question, in writing, before you pay anyone:**

> "Is this a **safety match conforming to IS 2653**, or is it a **colour/star match**?
> Please confirm in writing that it is a safety match. Send your BIS licence number."

**Get that in WhatsApp. Screenshot it. Keep it.**
If a vendor says "colour match" — **stop and come back to me.** Everything below changes.

**Confidence: BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY WITH PESO OR A CA.**
This is the single cheapest question you can ask and the most expensive one to skip.

# STAGE 1 — Buying plain trays from Sivakasi

### 1.1 The invoice they send you

**The rule:** a registered supplier must give you a proper tax invoice, and you must keep it.

**What you do — tell them exactly what to put on it:**
- Their **legal name, address and GSTIN**
- Your **name and full Pune address** (you have no GSTIN — that is fine and normal)
- **HSN 3605**
- **Description: Safety Matches**
- **Quantity, rate, total**
- **GST shown separately** — see 1.2
- **Place of supply: Maharashtra**

**The bit people miss:** you are unregistered, so this is an **inter-state B2C supply**.
The supplier charges **IGST**, not CGST+SGST. If their invoice says CGST+SGST, it is wrong
for you — ask them to reissue. **Confidence: CONFIRMED.**
Source: https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter7/section32_v1.00.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Cost: ₹0.**
**If you skip it:** you have no proof of what you own, no proof of what you paid,
and no defence if anyone asks where the matches came from.

### 1.2 The GST rate on the inbound invoice

**The rule:** matches are **HSN 3605**. The team is working to **5%**.

**Honest flag — this contradicts my earlier file.** In `legal-checklist.md` I wrote that
I could not confirm the rate and that older sources split it 5% (handmade) / 18% (other).
**The team has since settled on 5% and I am not fighting it** — multiple current sources
now show 3605 at **5%** post the 22 Sep 2025 rate overhaul.
Sources (seen 12 Sep 2026):
https://www.credlix.com/hsn-code/3605
https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/notification-9-2025-ctrate-complete-hsn-wise-gst-rate-chart-effective-22nd-sept-2025.html

**What you do:** when the first quote lands, **look at the GST% line on their invoice.**
That is the true answer, from a factory that files this monthly. Tell Finance the number.

**Cost: ₹0.** It is already in your ₹18 landed cost.
**If you get it wrong:** your MRP maths is wrong. That is a Finance problem, not a legal one —
**you are not registered, so you do not pay or collect this GST. You just absorb it.**
**Confidence: BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY on the first real invoice.**

### 1.3 The e-way bill for the truck

**The rule:** goods moving between states worth **more than ₹50,000** need an e-way bill.

**Who makes it: the Sivakasi factory, not you.** They are registered; they cause the movement.
You are unregistered and you do nothing.
Source: https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/faq.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter16/rule138_v1.00.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Does your order even cross ₹50,000?**
1,000 trays + 500 printed outers is about **₹12,500 total**, and the tray half is well under.
**So probably no e-way bill is needed at all.** Don't invent work.

**One line to say on the call:**
> "Consignment value is under ₹50,000, so I assume no e-way bill — confirm?"

If a later drop goes over ₹50,000, they generate it. If you ever move goods yourself
as an unregistered person, there is a **Form ENR-03 enrolment number** for exactly that.
Source: https://cleartax.in/s/e-way-bill-enr-03-enrolment-unregistered-person (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Cost: ₹0.**
**If you skip it (when it is needed):** the vehicle can be detained and the goods seized.
It would be the transporter's and supplier's problem first, but your matches are on that truck.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED on the ₹50,000 line. BEST UNDERSTANDING that the supplier files it.**

# STAGE 2 — Getting the outer box printed in Pune

### 2.1 Who is legally the manufacturer, and who is the packer

**The rule:** the printer is nobody. The factory is the manufacturer. **You are the packer.**

The Pune printer prints card. They are a job worker. They have no legal role in your pack.
The Sivakasi factory manufactured the matches.
**You** put the matches into your branded box with your name and an MRP on it.
That makes **you** the packer — and because your brand is on it, the **deemed manufacturer**.

Under **Rule 6(1)(a)** of the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, the
brand owner named on a pre-packed retail pack carries the label liability.
Source: https://1-comply.com/faqs-on-legal-metrology-act-and-packaged-commodities-rules/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**What you do:** file **Legal Metrology packer registration** with Maharashtra.
**Where:** https://www.vaidhmapan.maharashtra.gov.in
**Documents:** PAN, address proof, Udyam certificate, **a sample of the box artwork**.

**Cost: ₹500.** (₹100 to change it later.)
Source: https://www.vaidhmapan.maharashtra.gov.in/HomePages/newRegCert.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Timing:** you need the artwork before you can apply, and you need the artwork before
you can print. **So: finalise artwork → apply → print.** The application does not block
the print run, but do not sell publicly before it is filed.

**If you skip it:** penalties under the Legal Metrology Act for an unregistered packer.
It is a ₹500 problem that becomes a several-thousand-rupee problem.
**Confidence: BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY. One phone call to the Pune Legal Metrology
office settles it. Ask: "I'm the brand owner, factory is in Sivakasi — do I need my own
packer registration or does theirs cover me?"** This call is still on your week-1 list.

### 2.2 What the artwork must carry BEFORE it goes to print

**The rule:** everything in `packaging-label-rules.md`, and it must be right the
**first** time. Reprinting 500 outers is not free.

**Do not send a file to the printer until every one of these is true:**

- [ ] The words **"Safety Matches"** appear on the pack
- [ ] **Net Qty: [real number] Matchsticks (N)** — and you have **counted a real box**
- [ ] **MRP ₹99.00 (incl. of all taxes)**
- [ ] **Packed: MM/YYYY**
- [ ] **Marketed by:** your full name + **real postal address with PIN**
- [ ] **Manufactured by:** factory legal name + Sivakasi address — **in writing from them**
- [ ] **Country of Origin: India**
- [ ] **Customer Care:** a name, a phone you answer, an email you read
- [ ] Legal block is at least **1.0 mm cap height** (use **5–6pt**), letter width ≥ 1/3 of height
- [ ] Nothing on the pack implies smoking, tobacco, or any health/safety claim you can't prove

**Cost: ₹0.** It is ink you were already buying.
**If you skip it:** the label is the offence. Not the selling — the **printing**.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED** on the list and the 1.0 mm size for a display panel under 50 cm².
Source: https://nkgabc.com/principal-display-panel-how-to-declare-information-on-a-package/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

> **One-line instruction for the printer:** *"Back face is legally fixed — do not
> rearrange, resize, tint or abbreviate anything in that block. Front face is yours."*

# STAGE 3 — Labelling the finished pack

### 3.1 The single box

Full mandatory list is in `packaging-label-rules.md` and repeated in 2.2 above.
**Nothing has changed. Print the template.**

**Two things I want to say again because they are the ones that go wrong:**

1. **Count the sticks.** If the box says 50 and holds 40, that is a short-measure offence.
   Print the **guaranteed minimum**, not the average. Ask the factory for the minimum in writing.
2. **MRP includes GST.** ₹99 is the whole price. You can sell for less. **Never for more.**
   Not ₹99 + delivery-as-a-markup on the box price. Delivery is a separate line, not an MRP hike.

**Cost: ₹0.** **Confidence: CONFIRMED.**

### 3.2 The 3-box set — this is the bit that's new

Sales is selling a **3-box set at ₹279**. That is a **multi-piece package** in Legal
Metrology language: two or more identical retail-labelled pieces sold as one unit.

**What changes:**
- The **outer sleeve/band gets its own full declarations** — MRP of the **set**,
  net quantity of the **set**, and the packer/manufacturer details.
- Each **inner box keeps its own complete label**. You do not strip them.
- **Unit sale price is NOT required** on a multi-piece package.
Source: https://ssrana.in/articles/declarations-for-wholesale-packages-combination-and-multipiece-packages/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/advisory-declaration-mandatory-information-outer-retail-package.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**The set's outer must say:**
```
3 x SAFETY MATCHES
Net Qty: 3 Boxes (3 x [NN] Matchsticks)
MRP ₹279.00 (incl. of all taxes)
Packed: MM/YYYY
[Marketed by / Manufactured by / Country of Origin / Customer Care — same as the box]
```

**Sanity check on the price:** 3 × ₹99 = ₹297. You are selling the set at **₹279**.
That is **below** the sum of the individual MRPs. **That is allowed** — you may always
sell under MRP. It would only be a problem the other way round.

**Cost:** the sleeve is a print cost, not a legal cost. **Legal cost ₹0.**
**If you skip it:** an unlabelled outer sleeve is the same offence as an unlabelled box.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED** that multi-piece packs need outer declarations.
**BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY** on the exact wording for a 3-pack of matches specifically.

### 3.3 Boxes going to a café in a carton

A carton of 20 boxes handed to a café is a **wholesale package**. Under **Rule 24** it
needs, on the carton: **your name and address**, **what's inside**, and **how many
retail packs are in it**. That's all — no MRP needed on the carton.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/25849351/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**What you do:** write it on the carton with a marker. Genuinely, that is enough.
**Cost: ₹0.** **Confidence: CONFIRMED.**

# STAGE 4 — GST and invoicing

### 4.1 You are not registered. Here is what that actually means.

**The rule:** under ₹40 lakh turnover, goods only, one state = **no GST registration**.
Drop 01 is **₹29,700**. You are nowhere near the line.

**Now the correction you need to hear.**

> **You do not issue a "bill of supply". You cannot.**
> A bill of supply is a **GST document** issued by a **registered** person who can't
> charge tax (composition dealers, exempt goods). You are not registered at all.
> **Section 32 of the CGST Act** flatly prohibits an unregistered person from
> collecting any amount "by way of tax".
> Source: https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter7/section32_v1.00.html (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**What you issue instead: a plain invoice / cash memo with no GST on it anywhere.**

**Confidence: CONFIRMED.**

### 4.2 What your invoice must look like

**Put this on it:**
```
[BRAND NAME]
[Your full name], [full Pune address with PIN]
[phone] | [email]

INVOICE No. 001              Date: DD/MM/YYYY

Bill to: [Customer name, address, phone]

1 x Safety Matches (box of [NN])          ₹99.00
Delivery                                  ₹[XX]
                                   Total  ₹[XXX]

Paid by UPI / Cash    UPI ref: [XXXXXXXX]

GST not applicable — supplier not registered under GST
(turnover below the threshold).
```

**Take these OFF it. All of them:**
- Any **GSTIN** field (you don't have one)
- **CGST / SGST / IGST** lines
- The words **"Tax Invoice"** or **"Bill of Supply"**
- Any **HSN** column (harmless, but it invites the question)

**Do NOT write "₹99 + GST" or "inclusive of GST" on a customer invoice.**
The box says "incl. of all taxes" because that is the MRP rule. Your invoice says nothing
about tax except the one honest line above.

**Cost: ₹0.** A numbered Google Sheet or a free invoice app is fine.
**If you skip it:** collecting anything labelled "GST" without registration is an offence
under s.32 and you'd have to hand it to the government anyway.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED on s.32. BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY WITH A CA on the exact
wording of the "GST not applicable" line.**

### 4.3 When you must issue one at all

**Honest answer: there is no GST law forcing you to, because you're outside GST.**

**Issue one anyway, every single time, no exceptions:**

| Sale | Give an invoice? |
|---|---|
| Café / any business buyer | **Yes. They will ask. Always.** |
| Website pre-order | **Yes — email the PDF with the shipping note.** |
| Friend, cash, in person | **Yes.** Same numbered series. |

**Why:** it's your only proof of turnover, your only defence on a refund dispute,
and the thing a CA will ask for first. **One unbroken number series. Never reuse a number.**

### 4.4 Records to keep — and the good news

**The rule:** under **Section 44AA** of the Income Tax Act, an individual must keep
formal books only if **income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh** or **turnover exceeds ₹25 lakh**.
Drop 01 is ₹29,700. **You are not required to maintain books yet.**
Source: https://scripbox.com/tax/section-44aa/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Keep these anyway, in one Drive folder, for 6 years:**
- [ ] Every **supplier invoice** (Sivakasi, printer)
- [ ] Every **sales invoice** you issued
- [ ] **Bank / UPI statement** for the dedicated account
- [ ] The **factory's BIS + safety-match confirmation**
- [ ] The **Legal Metrology packer registration** certificate
- [ ] One **physical sample box** from every print run, in a drawer, with the date on it

**Cost: ₹0.**
**If you skip it:** when you register for GST later, or file your first real ITR,
you will be reconstructing a year from Instagram DMs. Don't.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED on the 44AA thresholds and 6-year retention.
BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY WITH A CA on your personal ITR position.**

# STAGE 5 — Selling on your own website

**The rule:** the second you take an order on your own site, you are an
**"inventory e-commerce entity"** under the **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020**.
You own the stock and you sell it directly. There is no "I'm too small" exemption.
Source: https://www.consumerprotection.in/rule-4-duties-of-e-commerce-entities/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/Consumer_Protection_E-Commerce_Rules_2020.pdf (blocked from this machine — cited from search results)

### 5.1 Seller identity — put this in the footer, on every page

- [ ] **Legal name** — your actual name, or the registered trading name
- [ ] **Address of your office** — the same real address that's on the box
- [ ] **Customer care contact** — phone and email
- [ ] **A named Grievance Officer** — name, designation, email, phone.
      **That is you.** Write "Grievance Officer: [Your Name]". It is one line.

**Confidence: CONFIRMED** that legal name, address, website details, customer care and
grievance officer must be displayed clearly.

### 5.2 The grievance clock

**Acknowledge a complaint within 48 hours. Resolve it within one month.**
**Confidence: CONFIRMED.**

**What you do:** a saved WhatsApp/email reply that goes out same-day. Two lines is enough.

### 5.3 Returns, refunds and cancellation

**You must publish, before checkout:** return policy, refund policy, exchange,
delivery and shipment terms, **who pays return shipping**, payment methods,
and the **total price as one figure with the break-up shown**.

**Two hard rules:**
- **You cannot charge a cancellation fee** unless you'd bear an equal charge yourself
  when you cancel. **Don't charge one.**
- **You cannot refuse a refund** on goods that are defective, spurious, or delivered late.

**Your policy, written for a matchbox — copy this:**
> Damaged or wrong box → **full refund or replacement, we pay return cost.**
> Changed your mind before dispatch → **full refund, no questions.**
> Changed your mind after delivery → **matches are a consumable; unopened boxes only,
> within 7 days, you cover return cost.**

**Cost: ₹0.** **Confidence: CONFIRMED** on the duties; **BEST UNDERSTANDING** that
7-days-unopened is a reasonable policy — that number is your choice, not a statute.

### 5.4 Pre-orders — say the date, or say there isn't one

A pre-order is you taking money for a box that does not exist yet. That is legal.
**Hiding that fact is not.**

**On the pre-order page, above the pay button, in normal-size text:**
> **This is a pre-order.** Boxes ship from **[DATE]**.
> **Pune hand-delivery only.** If we can't deliver to you, you get a **full refund**.

Sales' own rule already says: **never give a non-Pune buyer a delivery date.**
That rule is also the legally safe one. Keep it.

**If you skip it:** taking payment with no disclosed date, for an area you can't
deliver to, is exactly the "unfair trade practice" shape the CCPA acts on.
**Confidence: BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY WITH A CA.** The disclosure duty is confirmed;
the exact wording is mine.

### 5.5 Dark patterns — and the one on your site right now

**The rule:** the CCPA's **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023**
(in force **30 Nov 2023**) list **13 banned interface tricks** and treat them as
**unfair trade practices**. #1 on the list is **false urgency**.
Source: https://www.nls.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Dark-Patterns.pdf (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2268302&reg=3&lang=1 (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Your site has a live "X of 300 claimed" counter and a progress bar.**

**That is completely fine — as long as the number is true.**
The Website desk already built it so you type the real number in by hand, and their guide
already says "keep the counter honest". **They were right, and it is now a legal reason,
not just a nice one.** Enforcement position: scarcity and urgency claims must be
**demonstrable and supported by current evidence**.

**Never do these:**
- A counter that ticks up on its own
- "Only 3 left!" when there are 200
- A countdown that resets when the page reloads
- "X people are viewing this" if you're not actually counting them
- Adding anything to a cart the buyer didn't choose
- "No thanks, I don't want great matches" style guilt buttons

**Cost: ₹0.** It is free to tell the truth.
**If you skip it:** CCPA has already issued orders against named companies for exactly
false-urgency timers. You are small, but the rule is the rule.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED** that false urgency is a listed dark pattern and an unfair trade practice.

- **No GST.** Pune-only, under threshold. (Ship one box to Delhi and this changes — see 6.3.)
- **No trade licence** for selling from your own website.
- **No payment gateway licence.** Razorpay handles that.

# STAGE 6 — Getting 300 boxes to people in Pune

### 6.1 Why couriers refuse — the actual reason

**Matches are UN 1944, MATCHES SAFETY (book, card or strike on box),
Class 4.1 flammable solid, Packing Group III.**
Source: https://www.hazmattool.com/info.php?b=UN1944 (seen 12 Sep 2026)
Source: https://adr-tool.com/1275/un-1944 (seen 12 Sep 2026)

That classification is why **Shiprocket, Blue Dart, India Post and Ekart say no.**
It is not them being difficult. A normal courier network is not licensed, packed,
labelled or insured to move Class 4.1 goods, and most Indian parcel lines move by air.
**IATA permits basically one small packet of safety matches carried on a person** —
not 300 in a parcel.

**Nothing you can say will change their answer. Stop asking. Hand-deliver Drop 01.**
**Confidence: CONFIRMED** on the UN number and class. **BEST UNDERSTANDING** on each
individual courier's internal policy — that is their T&Cs, not a statute.

### 6.2 What hand-delivery legally requires

**The rule: almost nothing. This is the easy stage. Do not overthink it.**

Carrying retail-packed consumer safety matches in a backpack on a scooter is what every
kirana distributor in India does every day. The heavy hazardous-goods machinery in the
**Central Motor Vehicles Rules (Rules 129–137)** — TREM cards, hazard placards, trained
drivers, emergency info panels — is aimed at **bulk and tanker consignments**, not
retail packs in a bag.
Source: https://www.transportinfo.in/simba/cmvr/cmvr.htm (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Confidence: BEST UNDERSTANDING — VERIFY WITH A CA.** I could **not** find a written
small-quantity exemption naming a number of matchboxes. My reasoning is the real-world
one: this is retail distribution, not hazardous freight.

**What you actually do:**
- [ ] Carry the boxes **in the retail packs**, not loose, not in an open bag
- [ ] Keep them **dry and away from the exhaust**
- [ ] Carry **printed invoices** — one per delivery
- [ ] Get a **photo or a one-word WhatsApp "received"** as proof of delivery
- [ ] **Don't leave a bag of matches in a parked scooter in Pune sun all afternoon**

**Cost: ₹0** (the ~₹50/drop rider cost is Finance's line, not a legal one).
**If you skip it:** no legal consequence. But no proof of delivery = you lose the
"I never got it" argument, and under the e-commerce rules you'd have to refund.

### 6.3 If you use a rider / Dunzo / Porter type service

**Read their terms. Most prohibit flammable goods.** Sending matches through a service
whose T&Cs ban them isn't a crime — it is a **breach of contract**, and it voids any
cover if something goes wrong. **Confidence: BEST UNDERSTANDING — read the actual T&Cs.**

**The one that would genuinely change your legal life:**
> **The moment one box goes to a customer outside Maharashtra, that is an inter-state
> supply, and GST registration becomes compulsory regardless of turnover.**
>
> **So the Pune-only rule is not just a logistics rule. It is the thing keeping you
> out of monthly GST filings at ₹500–1,500 a month.** Hold that line for Drop 01.

**Confidence: CONFIRMED** (compulsory registration for inter-state supply of goods,
s.24 CGST Act — as already set out in `legal-checklist.md`).

# STAGE 7 — Advertising and Instagram

### 7.1 The tobacco line — do not go near it

**The rule: matches are not tobacco, and you are not covered by the tobacco
advertising ban. But you can walk into it by accident.**

**COTPA, 2003, section 5** bans all tobacco advertising, promotion and sponsorship.
Its rules also catch **"indirect advertisement"** — using a tobacco brand or name to
promote other goods.
Source: https://ssrana.in/articles/surrogate-advertising-india-law/ (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**So the hard rules:**
- **Never use a tobacco brand name, logo, or near-miss on one.** Instantly a COTPA problem.
- **Never show a cigarette, bidi, hookah, joint or anyone smoking** in your content.
- **Never write "for your smoke", "light up", "rolling", "stoner", "chillum", "smoke break".**
- **Never say the matches are for smoking.** Candles. Incense. Birthday cakes. Chai. Camping.

**Confidence: CONFIRMED** that COTPA s.5 bans tobacco advertising and that indirect
advertisement is defined in the rules. **BEST UNDERSTANDING** that clean match marketing
is unaffected — which it plainly is, since every match brand in India advertises.

### 7.2 The rule that will actually stop you — Meta's ad policy

This bites long before COTPA does.

**Meta prohibits ads for tobacco and nicotine products and related paraphernalia —
explicitly including rolling papers, pipes, hookahs and filters.**
Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/tobacco-related-products (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**Matches are not on that list.** But an ad reviewer who sees a matchbox shot next to
an ashtray does not read your legal file. **They just reject the ad, and repeat
rejections get the ad account restricted.**

**Practical rules for every paid post — give these to Brand:**
- [ ] **No ashtrays, no cigarettes, no rolling papers, no lighters-as-smoking-props** in frame
- [ ] Light a **candle** or **incense** in the demo shot. Every time.
- [ ] No **"smoke"**, **"light up"**, **"high"** wordplay in ad copy
- [ ] Remember your trademark class is **34 — "Tobacco; smokers' articles; matches"**.
      Platforms sometimes treat Class 34 goods with suspicion. **Give them no reason.**

**Cost: ₹0.** **If you skip it:** rejected ads, then a restricted ad account, then
you are rebuilding a Business Manager instead of selling boxes.
**Confidence: CONFIRMED** on Meta's written policy.

### 7.3 Influencers and seeding — the disclosure rule

**The rule:** if you give someone **anything** — a free box, money, a discount code with
a cut — and they post about it, **that post must be labelled as an ad.**

**ASCI expects:** **#Ad**, **#Sponsored**, **#Collab** or **#Partnership**,
in the **first two lines of the caption**, not buried in a hashtag block, in the
audience's language, on **every** platform the content runs on.
For **video**, a **verbal disclosure in the first 10 seconds** plus a text overlay;
on videos over 2 minutes the label stays visible throughout.
**Brand and influencer are jointly liable.**
Source: https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers (seen 12 Sep 2026)

**"I only sent a free box, I didn't pay them"** — **a free box is a material connection.**
It still needs the label. This hits your entire seeding plan.

**What you do:** put one line at the bottom of every seeding DM:
> "If you post it, please add **#Ad** or **#Collab** in the first two lines — it's an
> ASCI requirement and it protects both of us."

**Cost: ₹0.** **If you skip it:** ASCI complaints, and the brand is jointly on the hook.

### 7.4 Claims you may not make about a matchbox

**The rule:** no claim you cannot prove. Misleading claims are an unfair trade practice
under the Consumer Protection Act, and the CCPA can act on them.

**Do not print or post:**
- ❌ **"Eco-friendly" / "sustainable" / "carbon neutral" / "plastic free"** — unless the
      factory gives you paper backing it. Greenwashing is actively enforced.
- ❌ **"100% safe" / "child safe" / "non-toxic"** — it is a match. It sets things on fire.
- ❌ **"ISI marked" / "BIS certified"** — **only if the factory actually holds it**, and
      only using **their** licence number. Claiming BIS you don't have is a serious offence.
- ❌ **"India's first / best / No.1"** — you cannot substantiate it.
- ❌ **"Handmade"** — only if they genuinely are. It is also a different GST line.
- ❌ **Anything about burn time, strike count or windproofness** you haven't measured.

**You can say:** the design, the story, where it's made, what it's for, the count, the price.
**That is plenty.**
