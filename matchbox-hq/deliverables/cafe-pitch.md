# The Café Walk-In — Pune

You. A bag of sample boxes. A café you have never been to.
This is the whole script.

---

## BEFORE YOU WALK IN

- [ ] **Go between 3pm and 5pm.** Never at 9am, never 1–2pm, never after 7pm. Dead hours = they can talk.
- [ ] Ask one line at the counter: **"Who decides what you keep near the till?"**
- [ ] If that person isn't there — leave the sheet, get a name and a time, come back. **Do not pitch the barista.**
- [ ] Carry: **6 sample boxes**, **10 leave-behind sheets**, a pen, your UPI QR.

---

## THE 20-SECOND OPENING

Say this. Out loud. Exactly this length.

> "Hi — I make collectible matchboxes here in Pune. Small brand, first run of 500.
> People take them home and keep them, so they sit near the till and just… go.
> They cost you **₹60**, you sell at **₹99**. Twenty boxes, so **₹1,200** to try it.
> Can I leave one with you for ten seconds?"

Then **stop talking** and hand them a box.

Rules:
- **Hand them a box within the first 15 seconds.** The object does the selling, not you.
- Say **"₹60 in, ₹99 out"** in the first breath. Owners think in margins.
- Never say "collab", "brand partnership", or "synergy". Say **boxes**, **₹**, **counter**.
- Never lead with your story. Lead with their margin.

---

## THE 3 OBJECTIONS YOU WILL HEAR

### 1. "We don't buy stock from small brands / we don't do this."

> "Totally fair, you've never heard of me.
> So don't buy anything. Let me leave **15 boxes free**. You keep **₹30 on every one you sell**, you pay me nothing today, and I take back whatever doesn't move in a month.
> If they sell, we talk about a proper order. If they don't, you're out nothing but counter space."

*Why it works:* you removed 100% of their risk. Now "no" costs them nothing but also gains them nothing.

---

### 2. "How do I know they'll sell? / Who's actually buying matchboxes?"

> "Nobody buys them to light a stove. They buy them the way they buy stickers and fridge magnets — it's a ₹99 thing you pick up at the counter and keep.
> I've pre-sold **[X]** before a single box was printed.
> Start with **10 boxes**, not 20. If they're gone in three weeks, you'll know. If not, I take them back."

*Rule:* say your real pre-order number. If it's small, say the small number. **Never invent a number.** Café owners in Pune talk to each other.

---

### 3. "Leave your number, we'll think about it."

> "Sure. Can I just do one thing before I go —
> keep **three boxes** on the counter for two weeks. No paperwork, no money.
> If all three go, I'll come back on **[say an exact day]** and we'll talk about 20.
> If they're still sitting there, I'll pick them up and you'll never hear from me again."

*Why it works:* "we'll think about it" is a soft no. **Three free boxes converts it into a test with a date on it.** Always leave with a date, never with "sometime".

---

## WHICH ONE TO PUSH — AND WHY

> **Push Deal A. Wholesale. ₹60 × 20 = ₹1,200, paid today.**

Three reasons, plainly:

1. **Cash now beats more money later.** ₹44 today funds Drop 02. ₹53.30 in six weeks funds nothing. You have ₹20,000 total — cash speed is everything.
2. **Ownership makes them sell.** A café that paid ₹1,200 puts your boxes at the till. A café that got them free puts them in a drawer. This is the single biggest difference in sell-through.
3. **You only have 180 boxes for retail.** On consignment, 6 cafés × 15 = 90 boxes sitting on counters you don't control, for a month. That is half your retail stock frozen.

**Use Deal B only for this:** the first **2 or 3** venues, purely to get your boxes visible somewhere good so the next ten pitches start with *"we're already in a place in Koregaon Park."* That's what consignment is for — proof, not revenue.

**Cap it:** never more than **3 venues on consignment at once**, never more than **45 boxes total** out on consignment.

**The ladder to use in the room:**
1. Ask for **20 wholesale (₹1,200)**.
2. They hesitate → **10 wholesale (₹600)**.
3. Still no → **15 consignment**.
4. Still no → **3 free boxes on the counter + a date to come back.**

Never leave with nothing. One of those four always lands.

|---|---|

**Restock rule for venues that said yes:** walk in **every 10 days**. Count the boxes yourself. If 5+ moved, ask for the reorder right there, on the spot, standing up.

## WHERE TO GO — VENUE TYPES

Ranked. Start at the top.

1. **Specialty / third-wave coffee shops** — right customer, counter space, owner-operated.
2. **Cafés with a bar or a smoking terrace** — matches have an actual use there. Easiest yes.
3. **Concept / lifestyle stores and curated gift shops** — used to stocking ₹99 impulse objects.
4. **Bookstores and stationery/design shops** — collectible logic, same shelf as stickers and zines.
5. **Tattoo studios and barbershops** — young walk-ins, counters, owner decides in 2 minutes.
6. **Co-working space cafés** — daily repeat footfall, one decision-maker for the whole floor.
7. **Craft-beer bars and gastropubs** — good volume, but the decision-maker is hard to catch; go 4–6pm.
8. **Hostels and backpacker cafés** — travellers buy souvenirs; low price point works.
9. **Art galleries / event and flea-market stalls** — not permanent, but great for weekend volume.

**Avoid at launch:** chain cafés and franchise outlets (the manager cannot decide anything), supermarkets (needs GST paperwork, barcodes, distributor), anything where nobody at the counter owns the business.

| **Magarpatta** | Contained, walkable, high daily repeat. | Week 4 |
| **Hinjewadi** | Biggest IT volume but cafés are chain-heavy and far apart. | **Last.** Only after one area is proven. |

**Route rule:** do **one area per day**, on foot, **6–8 venues**. Never drive across Pune for a single pitch.

**Target:** 40 walk-ins → about **8–10 yes** → **6 stocked venues** → **180 boxes placed**.
